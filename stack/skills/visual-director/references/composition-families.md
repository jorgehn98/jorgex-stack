# Composition families

Twenty ways of composing a page or campaign, for proposing an open direction. A model asked to invent a look picks the category default every time, so use this list to force real distance between options: two directions from the same family are cosmetic variants.

This is a prompt for ideation, not a menu. Hold each family against the [subject inventory](good-design.md) and ask which one can carry something only this client has. Rule out any whose needs the project cannot meet. Justify the choice from the brief, combine or leave the list when the subject asks for it, and let the brand system win. The family is a way of composing; it still needs an idea from the subject.

A family may break a default in [web craft](web-craft.md) on purpose; say so, and the first screen must still say what this is. On a redesign that keeps the content, families that depend on new words are available only if the existing copy can carry them.

| Family | How it composes | Needs | Breaks when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name as monument | The name at viewport width or cropped, nothing competing; it returns inverted as the footer | A short name, or one that splits well | Over about 12 characters on one line; shrunk until it stops being monumental |
| Sentence as layout | A short sentence set huge, with a photo or product fragment inline in place of a word | A 4-7 word sentence worth that size | More than one inline gap per sentence |
| Pinned poster | Something stays fixed (title, frame, split name) while the rest changes beneath | Content for each state | The pin runs over about 3 screens or holds near-empty screens: reads as a stuck page |
| Colour chapters | Each section is one flat colour floor to ceiling, one type system and grid; the colour means the chapter | Colours from the subject | Over about 6 colours; cards on fields; neighbours that differ only in hue |
| The place carries it | One full-bleed photograph of the place is the argument; the name sits low like a film title; few words | A strong real photograph | The hero photo is weak: type cannot rescue it |
| Page as the object | The page is built as the subject's physical artefact: invitation, menu, ticket, sleeve, tabbed folder | A real artefact in the subject's world | It is a rectangle with a thin border instead of a shaped object |
| One motif | One shape from the subject governs buttons, masks and loaders | A shape you can source in one clause | A second motif appears |
| Product performs | Small, true, working fragments of the product respond to the pointer instead of describing features | A real product with real content | Fragments are fake or static; grey bars |
| Quiet instrument | Radical understatement: a modest headline that still says what this is, narrow column, small exact demonstrations, much untouched space | Strong exact content | Half-committed: one loud element ruins it; thin content reads as empty |
| Hero object | One photographed object enlarged until the viewport crops it, small text to one side or behind | A product photograph that survives enlargement | It becomes a centred catalogue thumbnail |
| Specimen catalogue | Each item alone on a flat field, same scale and angle, plainly named, like a field guide | At least 6 showable items | Three items: that is a feature row |
| Borrowed category | The subject art-directed as a livelier category (a money app as a clothing drop) | A category you can name in one word | The borrowed category is "tech startup" |
| Contact sheet | Dozens of small frames in strict rows, all text at one size; volume is the statement | About 20 or more images | Rows overrun the right edge and read as cut off |
| Editorial report | A printed report: header image, tight enormous title, one strict text column, colour in solid blocks | Real long-form content | The axis wanders; applied to a subject that is not news |
| Loud flat poster | Saturated flat colour, the heaviest type, short direct phrases, cut-out photos | Copy that can be blunt | Control falls below the noise; more than 2-3 colours; rotation beyond 2-3 images |
| Split stage | Half the screen fixed (photo or object), a reading column scrolling, the image changing with the section | One image per section | Monotony: vary the reading half and break the split at least once |
| Dense tiles | A board of filled tiles edge to edge with narrow gaps, no hero or band sequence | Enough distinct items, each one thing | Icon and blurb in every tile; phone is the desktop board squeezed |
| Uneven photographs | Photographs at uneven sizes and positions like prints in a gallery, small text between | A set from one shoot | Edges off the column lines; offsets not drawn from a small set |
| Dark room | One dark field and lit things, like a gallery at low light | Low-light imagery | Glow or neon; running text under about 7:1 |
| Type-only statement | First screen in words, colour and space only; pictures arrive later, large and few | Words strong enough to stand alone | Four sections of heading and paragraph: that is a document |

Vocabulary for section shapes when checking variety on a page: list, split, statement, full-bleed, mosaic, table, strip, pinned, grid.
