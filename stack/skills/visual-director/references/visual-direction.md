# Visual direction

Ground the aesthetic in the subject before choosing colors, type or effects. This reference covers web, audiovisual and fixed graphics; the medium changes expression, not the method.

## One sentence of intent

Write one sentence: *This should feel like **[specific world, material or cultural reference]** because **[reason tied to audience and objective]**.* If it could be reused for five unrelated clients, it is not specific enough. Then inventory the subject and write the idea sentence in [good design](good-design.md) §1-2: the intent says how it should feel, the idea says what carries it.

## Known direction: clarify, do not reinvent

When the idea is already defined, clarify invariants, inspect the real references and materials, and refine within them. Connect specific characteristics of a reference to proposed decisions rather than copying a recognizable composition. Preserve the approved direction; reopen only what the user authorized to change.

## Open direction: two or three real options

When the idea is open, proactively produce two or three genuinely **distinct** concepts specific to the subject, and recommend one with concrete tradeoffs.

Derive them before writing any of them. Name the piece this category always ships and its predictable opposite, and keep both out. Then list six to eight concrete worlds, artifacts, places, rituals or printed things the audience knows by heart, each with its ground, its light, its lettering and the one signature that makes it recognizable; the world's graphic and screen traditions count, not only its physical objects. If four of them share one material family, the derivation stopped at the subject's most obvious artifact; dig until it spans three. The tools of the craft are not a world: a terminal, a timeline or a control panel handed to a piece about that work is a costume.

Each option states:

- central visual idea tied to the subject;
- palette, typography and composition;
- imagery, illustration or material treatment;
- motion/camera language, including intentional stillness;
- one defining moment or sequence, if the concept benefits from one;
- minimum candidate method and its main tradeoff.

Do not offer cosmetic palette variants of the same template, and do not delegate all the creativity to the user: each option must defend why it fits. On pages and campaigns, to force real distance between options, take them from different [composition families](composition-families.md) and rule out those whose needs the project cannot meet. If the user asks only for the recommendation, develop that one and name the rejected options with their reason.

## Make one thing characteristic

For web, the first viewport foregrounds the most characteristic thing in the subject's world. For video, define one signature shot, transition or motion idea a viewer could recall. Spend visual boldness there; keep supporting areas disciplined.

The first screen, first frame or first seconds carry the thesis, not an introduction to it. Memory test: if someone left after one screen or two seconds, what would they describe an hour later? If the honest answer is a mood, the concept has not committed.

## Typography and structure are visual material

Typography carries personality, not just copy: one family or two with clearly different roles, deliberate width, weight, tracking, line-height, case, alignment and line length. Structure (borders, dividers, numbering, cards, grids) should encode hierarchy, sequence, grouping, state or affordance, not decorate empty space.

## Avoid generated defaults

The registry of generic tells, why removing them is not enough and what the evidence supports live in [good design](good-design.md). All of them can be correct; the problem is using them as defaults when the subject does not ask for them.

## Motion is attention management

Non-user-triggered motion should be selective; one orchestrated sequence often reads as more authored than many independent effects. Good reasons to animate: explain a relationship, preserve spatial continuity, show cause and effect, direct attention, reveal hierarchy, represent progress or state, create rhythm, or make a moment land. Do not ban motion, and do not animate because a library makes it easy.

## Two passes

**Pass 1 — Direction**: palette, type, composition/layout, shape/material, imagery, motion/camera and one signature moment. **Pass 2 — Genericity critique**: apply the competitor test and the tells in [good design](good-design.md); what is genuinely specific; is the signature moment doing the work; what should be removed. Revise before implementation if it still reads as a template. Name the few defaults this direction deliberately excludes, so production does not drift back to them.

## Commit, do not dilute

Land the chosen direction whole, not a safer reading of it. Later passes clarify what the piece already is; they do not soften it back toward a familiar page, cut or poster. When torn between refined and committed, commit: in unattended work the safe rendition is the known risk. Feedback reopens the decision it contradicts and is not a licence to average the result.

## Copy and references

Use real or specific placeholder content; generic marketing phrases make a strong layout feel generic. For video, on-screen copy must be readable at the intended pace, and spoken narration should not redundantly compete with on-screen text unless that repetition is intentional.

When references help, explain the chain: **source/context → specific characteristic → proposed translation → reason it serves this subject**. A reference can be an artifact, publication, material, tradition, place or interface. Borrow a principle, not a recognizable composition. There is no required number of references; inspiration does not grant reuse rights.

For each reference, separate what to **keep** (a controllable quality such as structure, rhythm, density or tone), what to **change** to fit this subject, and what **not to copy** (logos, claims, prices, proprietary UI, copy, a recognizable composition). "Do the same style" means borrowing controllable qualities, not cloning. A measurable constraint ("one warm accent, no purple glow") steers better than an adjective ("premium"). Where real numbers are missing, leave a visible neutral marker rather than inventing a statistic.

## Redesign boundary

Before proposing a redesign, distinguish **preserve / reinterpret / unresolved** for brand, content, information structure, key interactions, assets and technical constraints. A request for a more premium result does not authorize replacing the brand or changing behavior; an explicit overhaul is not constrained to cosmetics. See [design record](design-record.md) for where this boundary lives.
